# Player experience

## Intended experience

The intended rhythm alternates between fast real-time movement and short, player-controlled tactical decisions.

- Normal play rewards spatial awareness and efficient movement.
- Automatic targeting reduces repetitive touch input.
- Enemy telegraphs make danger readable before impact.
- Tactical Pause gives precision without converting the complete game into turn-based combat.
- Stage-end Trait choices provide build direction and anticipation.
- Failure is consequential but does not erase previously committed Hero progression.

The player should feel immediate pressure during combat, meaningful progress during a Dungeon Level, growing anticipation throughout a Dungeon Arc, and long-term purpose across the Journey.

## Player motivation horizons

The experience must provide goals at several timescales:

| Horizon | Player motivation |
|---|---|
| Encounter | Survive the immediate threat and make effective movement and tactical decisions. |
| Stage | Complete the objective and earn the next Trait choice or progression opportunity. |
| Dungeon Level | Reach and defeat the Boss, enter the Exit Portal, and secure permanent progression. |
| Dungeon Arc | Discover what lies deeper in the location, follow the story, adapt to evolving tactical challenges, and complete the Arc objective. |
| Journey | Develop the permanent Hero, resolve the central conflict, and determine which Dungeon Arc comes next. |

The player should always have both an immediate objective and a visible longer-term destination.

## Progression through a Dungeon Arc

A Dungeon Arc should feel like one evolving adventure rather than a collection of unrelated Dungeon Levels.

Each Dungeon Level should:

- Advance the Arc's mission or story.
- Reveal a new location, clue, enemy behavior, or consequence.
- Preserve enough familiar rules for the player to use previously learned skills.
- Introduce or combine a limited number of tactical changes.
- Increase anticipation for the Arc's final destination and climax.

The experience should evolve through **introduction, practice, combination, twist, and mastery**. Not every Dungeon Level needs a completely new mechanic. Existing mechanics can gain new meaning through enemy combinations, arena layouts, environmental pressure, objectives, and resource constraints.

## Clarity

The player must always understand:

- The current target and Stage objective.
- The Hero's attack range and available actions.
- Immediate threats and enemy telegraphs.
- Tactical Pause availability.
- The current Dungeon Arc objective and progress toward it.
- The consequences of death, manual exit, or successful completion.
