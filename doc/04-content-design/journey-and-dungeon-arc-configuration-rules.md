# Journey and Dungeon Arc configuration rules

## Hierarchy

```text
Journey
└── Dungeon Arc
    └── Dungeon Level
        └── Stage
            └── Encounter
```

A **Journey** is the complete long-term structure connecting multiple Dungeon Arcs. A **Dungeon Arc** is a connected mission or story spanning a configured range of Dungeon Levels. The initial design target is ten Dungeon Levels per major Arc, but the exact count remains configurable when an approved Arc requires a different length.

Concrete Journeys, Dungeon Arcs, Dungeon Levels, and story beats are authored in Content Studio. This document defines the configuration contract they must satisfy.

## Purpose

The Arc gives individual Dungeon Levels a shared destination and makes progression meaningful beyond numbers. The player should always understand:

- what they are trying to achieve;
- why they must enter the next Dungeon Level;
- what has changed since the previous level;
- what mystery or threat remains unresolved;
- what eventual climax or relic they are approaching.

## Required Arc identity

Every Dungeon Arc defines six connected design areas:

| Area | Requirement |
|---|---|
| **Story** | Premise, conflict, mystery, escalation, midpoint revelation, and climax. |
| **Goal** | One clear long-term player objective, such as finding a relic beneath a castle. |
| **Graphic design / visual identity** | Architecture, color script, lighting, materials, UI motifs, atmosphere, and environmental storytelling. |
| **Enemy ecosystem** | Primary enemy family, supporting roles, Elites, Bosses, and how the ecosystem develops through the Arc. |
| **New tactical idea** | One signature tactical mechanic introduced, practised, combined, twisted, and examined by the Arc finale. |
| **Climax and payoff** | Final location, Boss test, story resolution, relic or equivalent payoff, and connection to the next Arc. |

## Arc-level player loop

```text
Receive Arc mission
→ Enter the next Dungeon Level
→ Discover a story clue or environmental change
→ Learn or apply the Arc's tactical language
→ Defeat the level guardian or Boss
→ Enter the Exit Portal and secure progress
→ Return to Camp for a short story beat
→ Understand the next destination
→ Descend farther into the Arc
→ Confront the Arc Boss and recover the objective
→ Continue the Journey through the next Dungeon Arc
```

## Ten-level pacing model

| Position | Narrative function | Gameplay function |
|---:|---|---|
| DL 1 | Hook and entry | Establish familiar rules and Arc identity. |
| DL 2 | First escalation | Introduce the signature tactical mechanic safely. |
| DL 3 | Investigation | Practise the mechanic with a new enemy role. |
| DL 4 | First revelation | Combine it with a familiar pressure. |
| DL 5 | Midpoint guardian | Test understanding and reveal a deeper problem. |
| DL 6 | Consequence | Change the environment or objective using known rules. |
| DL 7 | Deeper descent | Add a supporting tactical variation. |
| DL 8 | Truth revealed | Twist the signature mechanic without changing its fundamentals. |
| DL 9 | Climax preparation | Combine the Arc's learned threats and prepare the final decision. |
| DL 10 | Arc climax | Boss examination, objective recovery, and narrative payoff. |

## Novelty budget

Each Dungeon Level should feel distinct without becoming a different ruleset.

- Approximately 70% familiar systems and 30% new or recombined challenge is the initial playtest target.
- Introduce no more than one completely new headline mechanic in one Dungeon Level.
- Use at most one supporting variation until the headline mechanic is understood.
- Reuse known enemies in new combinations before adding more enemy types.
- The Boss must examine the Arc's tactical language rather than ignore it.

## Tactical progression

The signature tactic follows this sequence:

```text
Introduce
→ Practise
→ Combine
→ Twist
→ Mastery examination
```

Once learned, the mechanic can enter the wider Journey tactical vocabulary and reappear in later Arcs in controlled combinations. The player therefore accumulates mastery while each Arc still has a recognizable identity.

## Story-delivery rules

Story delivery should remain concise and mobile-friendly:

- environmental changes;
- short dialogue or Camp conversations;
- inscriptions, journals, prisoners, keys, and artifacts;
- Boss introductions and conclusions;
- visible progress toward the Arc objective.

Each Dungeon Level should answer one question, create one new question, and point clearly toward the next destination.
